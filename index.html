The clause that decided it

Clause 13 of the client's standard form removed breaches of confidentiality and of intellectual property rights from every limitation of liability - then stated that liability was otherwise unlimited. On a fixed fee, that is unlimited exposure. The counter-draft caps it at 100% of fees. Deleted text in red, inserted in blue.

DELETED like this   INSERTED like this   - Clause 13, tracked

13. LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

Nothing in this Agreement limits either party’s liability for breach of confidentiality, breach of intellectual property rights, death or personal injury caused by negligence, fraud, or any liability that cannot be limited by law.

Subject to the above, each party’s total aggregate liability is unlimited.arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, whether in contract, tort or otherwise, shall not exceed 100% of the Fees paid or payable under this Agreement.

Neither party shall be liable for loss of profit, loss of revenue, loss of business, loss of anticipated savings, loss of data, or any indirect or consequential loss.

Clause 7 had the same shape: it assigned "all pre-existing tools, frameworks, components and know-how" to the client - the component library licensed to every other client - for a single project, with a "full title guarantee" a supplier cannot give over third-party works.

What was checked - and what was not

No case citation is relied on. Each proposition is traced to a statutory provision and carries an express status. Four section numbers could not be confirmed from a primary source at the time of drafting, and the memo says so rather than dressing them up as verified.

Method

1. Both versions compared clause by clause; every change described and classified. 2. Each clause scored 1-5 for the supplier's exposure in each version, with a written fallback position. 3. Legal propositions traced to statutory provisions with an explicit verification status. 4. Assembled into a memo addressed to the client, not to a file.

The reusable output is the scoring column, not the redline: it turns a list of edits into a negotiation order - three must-hold clauses, seven to protect margin, four tradeable, four give-aways.
